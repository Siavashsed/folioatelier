## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why do most personal finance tips not work for freelancers and people with irregular income?

Because nearly all general finance advice quietly assumes a steady paycheque. Budgeting, the 50/30/20 rule, 'save 10% of what you earn' all depend on knowing roughly what will arrive and when. If your income is lumpy, those rules break the first time a client pays late.

The core problem is that a budget is a record of where money already went. It describes the past. With irregular income, the real risk is in the future: the gap between invoices, the month that lands at a third of normal, the tax bill that arrives after you've spent the money.

I ran into this in March 2021 while working from a rented studio in Leeds. A $4,100 invoice came in eleven weeks late. Throughout the wait I felt comfortable, because my balance looked fine. In reality most of it was already owed to tax, rent and suppliers. I just hadn't admitted it.

What actually works is deciding what your money does before it arrives. A simple order of operations:

1. Tax share comes off the top, every time, before you see it as yours.
2. Cover a fixed floor: the minimum you need each month for rent, food, tools and insurance.
3. Fund a buffer large enough to carry you through a late or missing payment, ideally two to three months of the floor.
4. Pay yourself a steady 'wage' from the buffer, so your personal spending doesn't swing with your invoices.
5. Only then spend or save the remainder.

The mindset change matters most. Stop asking 'how much do I have?' and ask 'how much of this is already spoken for?'
