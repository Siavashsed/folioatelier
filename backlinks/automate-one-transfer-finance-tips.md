## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the single best financial habit to start with if you have irregular income as a freelancer or artist?

If I had to pick one, it would be automating a single transfer on the day you get paid. Not a full budget, not a new app, just one automatic move of money into a separate account.

Here is why this works better than most advice. Irregular income makes planning hard, so most systems fail the first time a month goes sideways. They also depend on willpower, and willpower is lowest exactly when money is tight. Automation removes the decision entirely.

How to set it up:

1. Open a separate savings account at a different bank or at least one you don't look at daily. A little friction helps.
2. Pick an amount that feels almost too small. Two to five percent of each payment is plenty to start. Consistency matters far more than size.
3. Trigger the transfer when income arrives, not on a calendar date. If you are paid per project, move the money the same day the payment clears.
4. Leave it alone for at least three months before you judge it.
5. Only after it feels normal, raise the percentage or add a second transfer, such as for taxes.

The real payoff is psychological as much as financial. I learned this after waiting 74 days on a $2,300 invoice while holding $212 in checking and facing rent. Once I had even a small buffer, I stopped taking bad clients out of desperation and could chase late invoices calmly.

Once the habit exists, everything else gets easier: tax set-asides, an emergency fund, even investing. But none of it sticks without the first automated step.
