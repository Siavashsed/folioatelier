## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How should artists with irregular income set aside money for taxes?

The short answer: decide the split before the money arrives, and move the tax portion out the same day it lands. Everything else is detail.

Here is why that works. With irregular income, the danger is not the slow months. It is the big payment. A $6,000 commission feels like a windfall, but a real share of it belongs to the government. If it sits in your checking account it gets spent on materials, rent or equipment. Then April arrives.

A practical system:

1. Pick a fixed tax percentage. In the US, self-employment tax is about 15.3% of net earnings, plus federal and state income tax. Many working artists end up setting aside somewhere between 25% and 30% of each payment. An accountant can refine the number after your first full year.

2. Open a savings account used only for taxes, ideally at a different bank so you don't see it every time you check your balance.

3. Transfer the slice the day the payment clears. Not weekly, not at month end. The money disappears during the delay.

4. Pay quarterly estimated taxes (Form 1040-ES). The deadlines fall in mid-April, mid-June, mid-September and mid-January. Paying at least 100% of last year's total tax (110% if your AGI was over $150,000) generally protects you from underpayment penalties, even if this year turns out bigger.

5. Split the rest on purpose: business costs, your own pay, and a buffer for lean months.

The habit matters more than the exact percentage. Moving the money the same day turns taxes from a yearly shock into a non-event.
