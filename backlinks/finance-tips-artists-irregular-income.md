**Title:** What finally fixed my lumpy artist income wasn't earning more. It was changing where the money lands.

Working artists get paid in lumps. Commissions, grants and print drops land at random times. The most useful change I've seen for that isn't a budgeting app. It's keeping the account where money arrives separate from the account you spend from.

Here's the setup. Every payment goes into a holding account, and nothing gets spent from it directly. On the 1st of each month you move the same fixed amount to your everyday account, whether the month was good or bad. That's your salary.

Why it works: a big payment stops feeling like a windfall. Without that buffer, a $6,000 commission gets slowly eaten by small upgrades and treat-yourself spending. Then a slow quarter hits and nothing is left.

A few things that make it stick:
- Set the salary from your leanest realistic months, not your average.
- Move tax money out the day a payment lands, before it looks spendable.
- Build 3 to 6 months of salary in holding before giving yourself a raise.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How should artists manage money when their income is irregular and comes in large, unpredictable payments?

The most effective change is structural, not motivational: stop spending from the account your clients pay into.

Irregular income causes trouble for two reasons. Big payments feel like windfalls, so they get spent faster than they should. Dry spells then feel like emergencies because nothing was held back. Monthly budgeting doesn't help much when you can't predict what a month will bring.

Here is the system I recommend:

1. Open a separate holding account. Every commission, grant, licensing fee and print sale goes there. Treat it as your business's reservoir, not your wallet.

2. Pay yourself a fixed salary. On the first of every month, transfer the same amount to your personal account, whether the month was great or terrible. Base that number on your leaner realistic months, not your average and never your best.

3. Set tax aside immediately. When a payment lands, move your estimated tax share to a separate account that same day. Money you've already set aside can't be accidentally spent.

4. Build the buffer before raising your pay. Aim for three to six months of salary sitting in the holding account. Once it's consistently there, you can raise your salary in small steps.

5. Review quarterly, not monthly. Lumpy income only makes sense across a longer window. Every three months, check whether your salary is sustainable against what actually came in.

The psychological effect matters as much as the math. A $6,000 commission stops being "extra money" and becomes several months of steady pay. A quiet quarter stops being a panic and becomes the situation the system was built for.
