## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why do most personal finance tips not work for freelancers and artists with irregular income?

Because most personal finance advice is built on a hidden assumption: that your income is predictable in both amount and timing. Rules like 50/30/20, 'save 20% of every paycheck,' or 'keep your rent under 30% of income' all take a stable monthly figure as their starting point. If you're a freelancer, artist or run a small studio, that figure doesn't exist in advance, so the rules either fail silently or leave you constantly feeling like you're behind.

I saw this first-hand helping manage a six-person print studio. The budget looked perfect on paper, but a gallery paid an $8,400 invoice 71 days late. Nothing in the plan accounted for the gap between earning money and actually receiving it, and that gap is where irregular earners get hurt.

The fix is to stop measuring monthly income and start measuring runway: how many months of essential expenses your cash on hand could cover. Once you track that, a few practical habits follow:

1. Pay yourself a fixed, modest wage from a buffer account on a set schedule, regardless of what came in that week.
2. Treat every incoming invoice as a refill of the buffer, not as spendable income.
3. Set your baseline wage from your lowest realistic months, not your best ones.
4. Keep taxes in a separate account and move a set share into it as each payment lands.
5. Build toward two to three months of fixed costs first, then expand toward six.

This turns a late payment from a crisis into a timing issue you already planned for. No app is required; a simple spreadsheet tracking buffer size and monthly fixed costs is enough.
