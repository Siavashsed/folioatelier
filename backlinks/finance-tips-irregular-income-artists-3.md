## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What are the best finance tips for artists with irregular income?

The most useful tip is also the least glamorous: stop budgeting against your average month and budget against your worst one.

Most standard budgeting advice assumes steady paychecks. Percentage-based systems (50/30/20 and the like) work when income is predictable. For artists, freelancers and collectives, income arrives in lumps. I once reviewed the books of a six-person print collective that had a $14,000 January followed by a $1,900 February. Their budget was built on the average, so the lean month broke it, since rent, insurance, supplies and software don't scale down with revenue.

Here's the approach that worked:

1. Calculate your floor. Add up fixed monthly costs (studio, housing, insurance, debt payments, tools) plus basic living expenses. That number is your real baseline.

2. Pay yourself a steady 'salary'. Route all income into one holding account and pay yourself a fixed amount from it each month, set at or near your floor. This smooths the swings.

3. Fill the floor, then the buffer. In strong months, first cover the floor, then build a reserve of three to six months of floor expenses. Only after that should extra money go to upgrades, investing or spending.

4. Set aside tax immediately. Move a percentage of every payment into a separate tax account the day it arrives. Irregular income makes quarterly estimates easy to forget and painful to catch up on.

5. Treat big months as funding, not raises. A windfall is usually paying for a future lean stretch.

6. Review quarterly. Adjust your floor as costs change, and track which income streams are reliable versus one-off.

The psychological benefit matters as much as the math. When your baseline is covered, you can say no to bad-fit commissions and make better creative decisions.
