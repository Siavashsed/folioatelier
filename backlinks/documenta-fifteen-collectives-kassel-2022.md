The curators, ruangrupa, ran the show on collective principles: shared resources, shared decisions, open spaces. Much of it worked better than a traditional curated exhibition.

The problem was governance. When controversy hit, there was no clear review process and no defined line of accountability. The model relied on goodwill, and goodwill is not a control system.

The lesson reaches well beyond art. Any organisation handing real authority to a collective or a flat team needs to pair that trust with explicit oversight: who decides, who reviews, who answers when something goes wrong.

Trust and structure aren't opposites. The best collective models need both.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What did documenta fifteen teach us about collectives running major art institutions?

The short answer: collectives can run a major exhibition, but trust has to be matched with oversight, and documenta fifteen showed what happens when it isn't.

I visited Kassel in late June 2022. The curatorial collective ruangrupa had built the show around lumbung, an Indonesian concept of communal resource sharing. Instead of a few star artists and a single curatorial voice, decisions and budgets were distributed across many collectives. In practice it felt open and social. Courtyards, kitchens and workshop spaces invited people to stay rather than file past objects.

That is the real achievement. It proved a collective can hold the biggest recurring exhibition in the world and make it feel different from the usual format.

The failure was structural, not conceptual. Collective models spread authority, which is their strength, but they also blur responsibility. When a controversy erupted over a banner on Friedrichsplatz, there was no clear mechanism for review, no obvious person or body empowered to respond quickly, and no agreed process for handling disagreement between the collective, the funders and the public. Everyone had been trusted, and no one was clearly accountable.

Three takeaways:

1. Distributed authority still needs defined accountability. Someone must be answerable.
2. Review should happen before opening, not in the middle of a crisis.
3. Funders and institutions that hand over control owe the collective support structures, not just money and trust.

None of this argues against collectives. It argues for building the scaffolding that lets them work under pressure. I wrote up my full visit and analysis here if you want more detail: https://siavashsed.github.io/folioatelier/documenta-fifteen-collectives-kassel-2022/

The broader point is that trust is a starting condition, not a governance model.
